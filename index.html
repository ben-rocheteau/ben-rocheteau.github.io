About

I am a Pre-Doctoral student in Economics at the Purdue Daniels School of Business, where my research sits at the intersection of banking and behavioral economics. I'm interested in how institutions and individuals respond to financial risk, and in bringing empirical rigor, both econometric and computational, to those questions.

Before starting the PhD, I completed an M.S. in Data Science and a B.S. in Economics and Mathematics at the Toulouse School of Economics. Along the way I worked on applied modeling projects at Banque de France, Moody's Analytics, and GeoSys, and taught macroeconomics and monetary history as a teaching assistant. That work gave me a foundation in causal inference, machine learning, and large-scale data pipelines that now informs how I approach research questions in banking and behavior.

Research

My research focuses on banking and behavioral economics — how financial institutions and the people within them make decisions under risk and uncertainty, and where standard rational-agent models fall short. I'm particularly drawn to questions that can be studied with both structural, causal-inference methods and modern machine learning tools.
